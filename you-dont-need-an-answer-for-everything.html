You Don't Need an Answer for Everything

There is freedom in admitting that some questions need time. Uncertainty can be uncomfortable without being something we need to immediately solve.

✦
01 — LETTING THE QUESTION REMAIN

Not every unanswered question is asking to be solved.

We are often taught to treat uncertainty as a problem. Something feels unfinished, so we begin searching for an explanation. We replay conversations, imagine different outcomes, and try to predict what might happen next.

Sometimes that search is useful. But sometimes the question itself simply needs more time than we are willing to give it.

A decision may become clearer after a few quiet days. A difficult experience may make more sense months later. Even our feelings can change once we stop demanding that they explain themselves immediately.

“Some things become clearer when we stop standing over them, waiting for an answer.”

02 — MAKING ROOM FOR UNCERTAINTY

Peace does not always come from knowing.

There is a particular kind of relief in saying, “I don't know yet.” It does not mean giving up. It simply means recognizing that understanding cannot always be forced into existence.

When we allow uncertainty to exist without immediately filling it with assumptions, there is more room to notice what is actually happening. We become less concerned with constructing the perfect explanation and more willing to observe.

This can change the way we move through ordinary moments. Instead of needing every silence to mean something, every delay to have a reason, or every ending to provide closure, we can let some things remain unfinished for a while.

The absence of an answer is not always an absence of progress. Sometimes waiting is part of the process of understanding.
