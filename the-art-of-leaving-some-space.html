The Art of Leaving Some Space

Empty corners are not necessarily unfinished corners. Sometimes breathing room gives the rest of an interior the chance to become more noticeable.

01 · The value of quiet space

Not every corner needs to be filled.

When decorating a room, it is easy to look at an empty wall, an unused corner or an open stretch of floor and immediately think something is missing. We are surrounded by interiors where every surface seems to have been given a purpose.

But an interior can become more comfortable when some areas are intentionally left alone. Space between objects gives the eye somewhere to rest. It also allows the pieces that matter most to feel more deliberate rather than competing with everything around them.

A room does not become complete when every corner is occupied. Sometimes it becomes complete when the right things have room to breathe.

02 · Creating breathing room

Let proportion do some of the decorating.

A single chair beside a window can have more presence than a crowded reading corner. A small table with nothing on it can make a room feel calmer than a surface covered with decorative objects. The difference is not always about the objects themselves. It is about the space around them.

This is where proportion becomes important. Before adding another piece, consider how the existing furniture relates to the room. Is there enough walking space? Can natural light move freely? Does the eye have a quiet point to settle on?

Leaving space does not mean creating a cold or empty interior. It means being selective. A few well-chosen pieces, natural textures and useful details can create a room that feels personal without feeling visually crowded.
